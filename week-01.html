Introduction and the core concept model

Six concepts that constrain each other. Get these right and the rest of the guide reads as one argument.

The guide opens by defining what business analysis is and then gives you the model everything else hangs from: the Business Analysis Core Concept Model. Six concepts — change, need, solution, stakeholder, value and context — none of which can be settled on its own.

That interdependence is the whole point and it is the thing most often skipped. A candidate who treats the six as a list to memorise will miss the questions built on them, because those questions turn on what happens to the other five when one of them moves. Spend this week getting comfortable holding all six at once.

Be precise about these

TermWhat it means
ChangeA controlled transformation of an enterprise in response to a need. Control and intent are what separate a change from a variation in conditions — a storm-driven spike in volume is not a change, a deliberate reorganisation is.
NeedA problem or opportunity to be addressed. Needs motivate change; they are not the same as the wants stakeholders express, and stating one as a solution is the most common error in the guide.
SolutionA specific way of satisfying one or more needs in a context. Note the last three words: the same solution can be right in one context and wrong in another.
StakeholderA group or individual with a relationship to the change, the need or the solution. Considerably wider than users — a regulator that constrains the solution is a stakeholder.
ValueThe worth of something to a stakeholder in a context. It can be a loss avoided or an obligation met, and it can be negative. Not confined to money.
ContextThe circumstances that influence, are influenced by, and provide understanding of the change. Not everything true about the enterprise — the test is whether a fact would change a decision.

What the exam does with this

Treating one concept as decidable alone. If the context turns out to be four distribution centres rather than one, the stakeholders and the solution both change with it.

Reading value as revenue. A compliance change with no return still delivers value: the licence retained, the loss avoided.

Reading stakeholder as user. Anyone with a relationship to the change qualifies, including parties who only constrain or test it.

Widening context until it means nothing. A description that includes everything discriminates nothing.

Before the session

  • Read chapters 1 and 2 of the guide.
  • Take a change you are working on now and write one sentence for each of the six concepts. Notice which one you find hardest — for most people it is value or context.
  • Bring an example where a change in context forced the solution to change.

Worth discussing

  • Which of the six is hardest to pin down on your own work, and why?
  • Give an example of value that was not money. How did you get it accepted?
  • Where have you seen a need stated as a solution, and what did it cost?
