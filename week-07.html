Requirements Analysis and Design Definition, part 1

The largest knowledge area on the exam. Specify, verify, validate — and know which is which.

RADD is thirty per cent of the blueprint, more than any other area, and it splits neatly into two halves. The first is specifying and modelling requirements, then checking them twice: verification and validation.

Those two checks are the most confused pair in the guide. Verification asks whether the requirement is well written — unambiguous, testable, consistent. Validation asks whether it delivers value and aligns to the business need. Both can be done with a review, so the question is never which technique was used; it is what the reviewers were asked to judge, and whether the right people were in the room.

Tasks in scope

TaskWhat it is for, and what to watch
7.1 Specify and Model Requirements
Express requirements and designs in a form fit for the audience: text, matrices, diagrams, models.Watch: The notation follows the subject. Hand-offs between people point to a process model; statuses point to a state model; ordered messages between components point to a sequence diagram.
7.2 Verify Requirements
Check the requirement is well specified and of sufficient quality to work from.Watch: A well-written requirement can still be the wrong one. Verification cannot detect that.
7.3 Validate Requirements
Check the requirement delivers value and supports the business objectives.Watch: Needs the business in the room. Testers verifying among themselves will pass obsolete requirements.

Be precise about these

TermWhat it means
VerificationAre we building it right — is this requirement well formed?
ValidationAre we building the right thing — does this requirement still deliver value?
Alternate and exception flowsWhat a use case carries beyond the happy path. Their absence is why so much gets discovered during testing.
Modelling notationDifferent notations answer different questions. A data flow diagram follows information; a process model follows work; they are not interchangeable.

What the exam does with this

Treating verification as sufficient. A third of a requirements set can be obsolete and still pass a quality review.

Specifying only the successful path and calling the requirement complete.

Choosing a notation because the team prefers it rather than because it expresses the subject.

Burying a volatile policy inside the workflow that applies it.

Before the session

  • Read 7.1 to 7.3.
  • Take one requirement and write its alternate and exception flows. Most people find more than they expected.
  • Bring a model you have drawn and be ready to say why that notation and not another.

Worth discussing

  • Who validates requirements in your organisation, and are they the right people?
  • Which notation do you reach for by habit rather than by fit?
  • Where have business rules ended up hard-coded, and what did the next policy change cost?
