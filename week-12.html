Perspectives

Five lenses on the same work. Not exclusive, not a house style, and examinable.

The final chapter describes five perspectives on business analysis: agile, business intelligence, information technology, business architecture and business process management. Each shades how the tasks and techniques are applied without replacing them.

Two things carry the exam questions here. First, perspectives are not mutually exclusive — a single change routinely has data, technology, process and capability dimensions at once, and choosing one lens leaves the others unexamined. Second, the approach follows the work rather than the team's habit: legislated rules that are fixed in advance do not want just-in-time elaboration, however the rest of the organisation runs.

Tasks in scope

TaskWhat it is for, and what to watch
11.1 Agile
Requirements elaborated just in time, in short cycles, with the analyst helping the team and the product owner understand each other.Watch: The analyst is not a proxy customer. Deferring detail is not the same as deferring the check that an item can be finished.
11.2 Business Intelligence
Specify the decision the information supports, then the data: sources, quality, latency and agreed meaning.Watch: Two correct figures for the same measure is usually a definition problem, not a data quality defect.
11.3 Information Technology
Analysis across the software life cycle, with strong emphasis on quality attributes and the existing landscape.Watch: Non-functional requirements drive the architecture. Leaving them to the technical team inverts the order.
11.4 Business Architecture
The enterprise as the unit of analysis: capabilities, value streams, information and organisation.Watch: Not solution or technology architecture. A capability view must stand without reference to systems.
11.5 Business Process Management
The end-to-end process as the unit of analysis and of ongoing management.Watch: A process is owned and measured continuously, not improved once by a project that then disbands.

Be precise about these

TermWhat it means
Progressive elaborationDetail added as it is needed. Not licence to skip establishing whether an item is ready to build.
LatencyHow current the information is. A first-order requirement in business intelligence work, set by the decision it supports.
Capability-based planningInvesting against what the enterprise must be able to do, rather than against departments or systems.
End-to-end processThe whole flow that produces an outcome. Improving a step that is not the constraint moves the queue, not the result.

What the exam does with this

Choosing a perspective by preference rather than by the work.

Assuming one perspective covers a change that has four dimensions.

Treating a backlog as the record of the current state.

Improving a process fragment and reporting it as an end-to-end gain.

Before the session

  • Read chapter 11.
  • Take a change you are on and name every perspective that applies. Most have more than one.
  • Bring an example where the approach was chosen by habit rather than by fit.

Worth discussing

  • Which perspective does your organisation default to, and what does it miss?
  • Where have you seen a backlog asked to do the job of a requirements record?
  • Who owns an end-to-end process in your organisation — genuinely, not nominally?
