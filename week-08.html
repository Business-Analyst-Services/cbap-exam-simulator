Requirements Analysis and Design Definition, part 2

Architecture, options and the recommendation. Where analysis turns into a decision.

The second half of RADD structures the requirements as a whole, defines the design options that could meet them, and recommends one on the basis of value. It is where the analysis work of the previous weeks is brought to a point.

The recurring examinable distinction is between analysing potential value and planning delivery. Producing a work breakdown, a schedule or a resourcing profile for the options is project management. The analyst owes a value-based recommendation, and being asked for a delivery plan instead is one of the most frequently set traps in the guide.

Tasks in scope

TaskWhat it is for, and what to watch
7.4 Define Requirements Architecture
Structure the requirements so they fit together, cover the need and can be traced.Watch: Gaps and inconsistencies between requirements are found here, not in a single requirement's wording.
7.5 Define Design Options
Identify ways of meeting the need, including improving the current state, and describe each well enough to compare.Watch: Doing nothing, and improving what exists, are options. A single costed option is not a set.
7.6 Analyze Potential Value and Recommend Solution
Assess the value each option could deliver and recommend one.Watch: This is the value question, not the delivery question. Watch for a plausible option that is beaten, or that fails against the cost of capital.

Be precise about these

TermWhat it means
Requirements architectureThe requirements as a structured whole, with the relationships between them, rather than a list.
Design optionA way of meeting the need. Must include enough detail to be compared, and the set must include the alternatives that were genuinely considered.
Total cost of ownershipAcquisition plus operating, support and disposal, over the whole life. The purchase price is rarely the decision.
Trade-offWhat is given up by choosing one option. A recommendation without one has usually not been analysed.

What the exam does with this

Producing a delivery schedule when a value recommendation was owed.

Specifying what crosses a boundary before agreeing where the boundary is.

Assessing the product and forgetting the supplier behind it.

Leaving quality attributes to be settled by the technical team during design, after the architecture is chosen.

Before the session

  • Read 7.4 to 7.6.
  • Take a recent solution decision and write the option set as it should have been — including doing nothing.
  • Bring a set of non-functional requirements and check whether every one carries a number.

Worth discussing

  • How are design options generated in your organisation, and by whom?
  • When did you last see doing nothing seriously assessed?
  • Who decides the quality attributes where you work?
