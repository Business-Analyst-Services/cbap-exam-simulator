Strategy Analysis, part 1

Understand where the enterprise is before describing where it should go. Diagnosis before design.

Strategy analysis is where a change is justified rather than specified. The first half is current state and future state: understanding the enterprise as it is, why it underperforms, and then describing the state that would satisfy the need.

The discipline this week is not jumping. Analysing the current state is diagnosis, and an item that moves from a recurring failure straight to a fix is testing whether you stopped to find the cause. Equally, the future state is a description of a state, not a plan for getting there — that comes next week.

Tasks in scope

TaskWhat it is for, and what to watch
6.1 Analyze Current State
Understand the enterprise as it is: its needs, capabilities, structure, culture, and the external environment it operates in.Watch: This task diagnoses. It does not design, and it does not choose. If the answer proposes a solution, check whether the cause has been established.
6.2 Define Future State
Describe the state that would meet the need, with objectives set well enough to tell whether you got there.Watch: A future state is a description, not a delivery plan. Objectives need to be measurable or the change cannot be evaluated later.

Be precise about these

TermWhat it means
Business needThe problem or opportunity of consequence to the enterprise. Everything in strategy analysis traces back to it.
CapabilityWhat the enterprise is able to do, independently of who does it or how. Capabilities cut across departments, which is why departmental fixes leave enterprise symptoms untouched.
Potential valueThe value a future state could deliver. Assessed before commitment, and revisited when the context moves.
Solution scopeThe boundary of what a solution will address. Defined here well enough to estimate, refined later.

What the exam does with this

Designing the future state while describing the current one.

Confusing internal weakness with external threat. Lacking a capability is a weakness; a competitor exploiting that lack is the threat.

Comparing yourself only with yourself. A claim about being uncompetitive needs an outside reference point.

Framing an enterprise-wide symptom as a departmental process problem, then fixing three departments and wondering why nothing moved.

Before the session

  • Read 6.1 and 6.2.
  • Sketch a capability view of your own organisation — six to ten capabilities, graded for performance and importance. Notice how different it looks from the org chart.
  • Bring a current-state finding you had to defend to someone who wanted to skip to the solution.

Worth discussing

  • How does your organisation decide a need is worth acting on?
  • Where have you seen a solution chosen before the need was agreed, and how did it end?
  • Capability or process — which framing gets more traction with your executives, and why?
