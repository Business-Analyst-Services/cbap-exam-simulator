Requirements Life Cycle Management

Keeping requirements usable from the moment they appear to long after they are delivered.

This chapter is about custody. Requirements are traced to what justifies them and what implements them, kept current as things change, ordered so the most valuable work comes first, assessed when someone proposes a change, and formally approved.

The examinable heart of it is prioritisation. Value is one basis among several, and it is not always the governing one. A regulatory deadline, a dependency, a risk or an implementation constraint can each outrank it, and an item that hands you a statutory date and then asks you to rank on business value is testing exactly that.

Tasks in scope

TaskWhat it is for, and what to watch
5.1 Trace Requirements
Maintain the relationships between requirements, designs, solution components and the objectives they serve.Watch: Tracing is what lets you answer 'why does this exist' and 'what breaks if it changes'.
5.2 Maintain Requirements
Keep requirements accurate and reusable over time, including after delivery.Watch: A backlog of items that describe changes is not a statement of the current state. Both are needed.
5.3 Prioritize Requirements
Order requirements against an agreed basis.Watch: The basis matters more than the ranking. Value, risk, dependency, regulatory deadline, cost and difficulty are all legitimate bases.
5.4 Assess Requirements Changes
Evaluate a proposed change for its effect on value, on other requirements, and on the work.Watch: The assessment is of impact, not of whether the requester is entitled to ask.
5.5 Approve Requirements
Obtain agreement from the people with authority to give it.Watch: Approval needs the right authority, which means knowing where decision rights actually sit.

Be precise about these

TermWhat it means
TraceabilityThe relationships that let a requirement be followed up to its justification and down to its implementation.
Basis for prioritisationWhat the ordering is against. Naming it is what makes a ranking defensible to whoever loses out.
Backlog managementThe ongoing custody of the list. Distinct from prioritisation, which is the act of ordering it.
BaselineAn approved set, held so that later change can be assessed against something fixed.

What the exam does with this

Ranking on value when a dependency or a statutory date governs.

Re-prioritising a list a third of which is obsolete, and producing a well-ordered list of the wrong work.

Confusing an issue with a risk. An issue is already happening.

Approving with whoever is available rather than whoever holds the mandate.

Before the session

  • Read chapter 5.
  • Look at your current backlog and estimate what share of it no longer represents a live need.
  • Bring a prioritisation decision that was overturned, and be ready to say on what basis it had been made.

Worth discussing

  • What is your organisation's real basis for prioritisation, as opposed to its stated one?
  • How would you answer an auditor asking what the current rule is and when it last changed?
  • Who can approve a requirement where you work, and does everyone agree on that?
