Solution Evaluation

Did it work, and if not, is the fault inside the solution or in the enterprise around it?

Solution evaluation asks whether the thing that was built is delivering the value that justified it. It runs on solutions already in use, and on ones still in construction, and it is the knowledge area candidates most often underestimate because it looks like reporting. It is not.

The distinction that carries this chapter is where a limitation sits. A solution can be working exactly as designed and still deliver nothing, because the enterprise around it has not changed. Telling those two apart decides which task you are in and what you would recommend, and the exam tests it repeatedly.

Tasks in scope

TaskWhat it is for, and what to watch
8.1 Measure Solution Performance
Decide what will be measured and how, so performance can be judged rather than asserted.Watch: Without a baseline there is no improvement to claim. Measures also need targets and thresholds, or they produce reporting rather than management.
8.2 Analyze Performance Measures
Interpret the measures against the expected value and say what they mean.Watch: Gross benefit is not realised benefit. Run cost, and any cost that has not yet stopped, come off before anything is claimed.
8.3 Assess Solution Limitations
Find what is constraining performance inside the solution itself.Watch: Defects, missing function, unusable design — things you would fix by changing the solution.
8.4 Assess Enterprise Limitations
Find what is constraining performance outside the solution: structure, culture, policy, capability.Watch: A perfectly built solution nobody is trained, incentivised or permitted to use is limited by the enterprise, not by itself.
8.5 Recommend Actions to Increase Solution Value
Say what should be done: adjust, replace, retire, or change the enterprise around it.Watch: Doing nothing is a legitimate recommendation and should appear when it is the right one.

Be precise about these

TermWhat it means
BaselineThe measured value before the change. Without it, improvement is an assertion. Establishing one is part of 8.1, not an afterthought.
Leading and lagging indicatorsA lagging indicator confirms an outcome after it is too late to change it. A dashboard of only lagging measures cannot support intervention.
Non-cashable benefitHours released that never leave the budget. Real, but not a saving until establishment falls, bought-in spend drops, or the capacity absorbs work you would otherwise have paid for.
Solution limitationA constraint within the solution. Contrast with an enterprise limitation, which sits in the organisation around it.

What the exam does with this

Reading gross benefit as net. Run cost and double-running cost come off first.

Optimising one dimension because the measures that would show the cost are absent — a balanced scorecard exists to catch exactly that.

Calling an enterprise limitation a solution defect, and rebuilding something that works.

Claiming hours saved as money when headcount and budget are unchanged.

Before the session

  • Read chapter 8.
  • Take a solution you have delivered and ask what its baseline was. If you cannot answer, that is the lesson.
  • Bring an example where the solution worked and the benefit did not arrive.

Worth discussing

  • Who owns benefit realisation in your organisation, and does it survive go-live?
  • Have you seen a non-cashable benefit claimed as a saving? What happened at audit?
  • Where did an enterprise limitation get mistaken for a system problem?
