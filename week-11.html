Business Analysis Planning and Monitoring, part 2

Governance, information management, and improving the analysis itself.

The second half of chapter 3 covers how decisions about requirements get made and changes controlled, how the analysis information is stored, traced and reused, and how business analysis performance is assessed and improved.

Governance and information management are easily blurred. Governance is about authority: who approves, who is consulted, how change is controlled. Information management is about the artefacts: where they live, how they are traced, how long they are kept and how they get reused. A question about who may approve a change is governance; a question about where the approved version lives is information management.

Be precise about these

TermWhat it means
GovernanceWho decides, who is consulted, and how change is controlled.
Information managementHow the analysis information is organised, traced, retained and reused.
Performance measure for analysisA measure of the analysis work — churn, rework, cycle time, defect origin — not of the solution.

What the exam does with this

Answering a governance question with an information management answer, or the reverse.

Deferring a lessons learned session until benefits data matures, by which time the team has gone.

Measuring the solution when the task asks about the analysis.

Treating a follow-through failure as a facilitation problem.

Before the session

  • Read 3.3 to 3.5.
  • Find out who is actually authorised to approve a requirement change on your current work. It is often not who you assume.
  • Bring a lessons learned session that produced nothing, and be ready to say why.

Worth discussing

  • Where does your analysis information live, and could someone else find it in a year?
  • How is business analysis performance measured in your organisation, if at all?
  • What would you change about how change is controlled where you work?
