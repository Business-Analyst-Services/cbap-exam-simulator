Business Analysis Planning and Monitoring, part 1

Planning the analysis itself. The knowledge area that governs all the others.

Chapter 3 is business analysis about business analysis: how the work will be done, who will be involved, how decisions get made, how information is managed, and how performance is improved. It is deliberately worked late in the twelve weeks, because it is far easier to plan work you now understand.

The first half covers the approach and stakeholder engagement. The distinction to hold is between analysing stakeholders and planning engagement with them: identifying who they are and positioning them by influence and interest is analysis; deciding how often, how formally and by what means you will deal with each is the plan. Producing a map is not having a plan.

Tasks in scope

TaskWhat it is for, and what to watch
3.1 Plan Business Analysis Approach
Decide how the analysis will be done: predictive, adaptive or a mix, with the deliverables and formality that follow.Watch: The approach follows the work. Fixed, externally determined requirements argue for up-front specification whatever the house style.
3.2 Plan Stakeholder Engagement
Decide how you will work with each stakeholder, based on who they are and what they need.Watch: Analysis identifies and positions them; the plan says what you will actually do about each.

Be precise about these

TermWhat it means
Predictive and adaptiveTwo ends of a spectrum, not a binary. Most real work sits somewhere between, and the choice follows the uncertainty in the requirements.
Stakeholder analysisIdentifying who is affected and positioning them by influence, interest and attitude.
PersonaA characterised user group. Needed when a cohort contains materially different needs that an average would hide.
RACIOne way of recording who is responsible, accountable, consulted and informed. A device inside the engagement plan, not the plan itself.

What the exam does with this

Producing a stakeholder map and calling it an engagement plan.

Designing for an average user in a cohort whose groups differ materially.

Choosing the approach by organisational habit rather than by the nature of the work.

Planning engagement when the real problem is that nobody can say where decision rights sit.

Before the session

  • Read 3.1 and 3.2.
  • Write the approach for a piece of work you are on now, in half a page. Note where you had to guess.
  • Bring a stakeholder who was missed on a previous engagement, and what it cost.

Worth discussing

  • Who decides the analysis approach in your organisation — the analyst, or the method?
  • How do you handle a stakeholder with high influence and no interest?
  • Where have decision rights moved without anyone recording it?
