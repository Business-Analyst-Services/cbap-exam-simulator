Elicitation and Collaboration

Choosing the instrument is most of the skill. Depth or breadth, stated or observed, discussion or decision.

Elicitation is drawing out information; collaboration is keeping the people involved engaged while you do it. The chapter covers preparing, conducting, confirming what you got, communicating it, and managing the relationships that make any of it possible.

Most questions here turn on picking the right instrument for the situation. Interviews go deep with a few people and can ask the follow-up question; surveys go wide and cannot. Observation catches what people cannot articulate or do not realise they do. Workshops produce an agreed output; focus groups produce opinion. Read the scenario for what is actually needed and the choice usually settles itself.

Tasks in scope

TaskWhat it is for, and what to watch
4.1 Prepare for Elicitation
Scope the activity, pick the technique, arrange the logistics and the participants.Watch: Document analysis belongs here. Time with a scarce expert is wasted on questions the documents already answer.
4.2 Conduct Elicitation
Draw the information out.Watch: The technique has to match the information sought — tacit expertise is not reachable by questionnaire.
4.3 Confirm Elicitation Results
Check what you captured against other sources and with the people who gave it.Watch: Two records that appear to conflict are often one word carrying two meanings.
4.4 Communicate Business Analysis Information
Get the information to stakeholders in a form they can act on.Watch: The form follows the audience and the decision, not the analyst's preference.
4.5 Manage Stakeholder Collaboration
Keep stakeholders engaged through the work.Watch: Disengagement usually follows a failure of follow-through, not of facilitation. Concerns raised with no owner teach people not to raise them.

Be precise about these

TermWhat it means
Tacit knowledgeExpertise the holder has never articulated. Reachable by interview and observation, not by a fixed instrument.
Self-selected sampleThe people who chose to respond. A banner survey on a portal reaches those the portal worked for, which is why a low response rate can be flattering rather than merely thin.
WorkshopA facilitated session with a stated output, the people who can agree it, and a scribe. Missing any of those and it is a meeting.

What the exam does with this

Reaching for breadth when the need is depth, or the reverse.

Believing the documented procedure over the work as performed.

Treating a vocabulary dispute as a requirements conflict.

Blaming facilitation when the real failure is that nothing happened after the session.

Before the session

  • Read chapter 4.
  • Write the first five questions you would put to a subject-matter expert you had ninety minutes with, and mark which ones a document could answer.
  • Bring a session that went badly and be ready to say which instrument would have suited it better.

Worth discussing

  • What is the most useful elicitation you have ever done, and why did it work?
  • How do you handle a stakeholder who agrees in the room and objects afterwards?
  • Where does information go to die in your organisation?
