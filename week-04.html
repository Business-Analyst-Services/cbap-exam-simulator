Strategy Analysis, part 2

Risk and the change strategy. This is the week the numbers show up.

Having described where the enterprise is and where it should be, the second half of strategy analysis assesses what could go wrong on the way and settles how the change will be made. This is where financial analysis, decision analysis and risk work sit, and where most candidates find the arithmetic they have been avoiding.

The exam leans hard on two things here. First, that mitigation changes a risk rather than closing it — residual risk has to be accepted explicitly. Second, that a positive return is not the same as the best option: expect to be shown something profitable that is beaten by an alternative, or that fails against the cost of capital.

Tasks in scope

TaskWhat it is for, and what to watch
6.3 Assess Risks
Identify uncertain events that could affect value, assess likelihood and impact, and settle a response for each.Watch: A risk is an uncertain future event; an issue is already happening. Responses are avoid, transfer, mitigate, accept or increase — not simply escalate.
6.4 Define Change Strategy
Choose the approach to the change: the options, the recommended one, the gaps to be closed and the transition states.Watch: The strategy explains how the enterprise will get from here to there, including what happens in between. Options that were rejected belong in it.

Be precise about these

TermWhat it means
Residual riskWhat remains after a response is applied. Mitigation moves a risk on the grid; it does not remove it from the grid, and accepting what remains must be a decision on the record.
Expected monetary valueEach outcome weighted by its likelihood. The figure that belongs in a business case, while the severe case belongs in the risk register.
Net present valueDiscounted benefits less discounted costs. Positive means the option beat the cost of capital — a raw surplus can still be a negative NPV.
Transition stateAn intermediate state the enterprise passes through. Real changes rarely go from current to future in one move.

What the exam does with this

Comparing undiscounted totals when the options differ mainly in timing.

Reading a ratio as a size. A 300% return on $10,000 is a smaller prize than 50% on $500,000.

Treating a short payback as decisive when the constraint is value rather than cash exposure.

Letting money already spent influence a forward-looking choice.

Closing a risk because a monitoring alert was added. Detection is not prevention.

Before the session

  • Read 6.3 and 6.4.
  • Work one NPV by hand: discount each year separately, then add. If you can do that, most of the financial questions become arithmetic rather than judgement.
  • Bring a business case you have written or reviewed, and be ready to say what it left out.

Worth discussing

  • How does your organisation set its hurdle rate, and does anyone check options against it?
  • Where have you seen sunk cost drive a decision?
  • Which risks get recorded in your shop, and which get carried as optimism?
